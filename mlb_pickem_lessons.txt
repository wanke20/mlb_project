MLB DAILY PICKEM FRAMEWORK
Lessons learned from 10 days of picks (April 30 – May 9, 2026)
================================================================

CORE PHILOSOPHY
---------------
Surface ERA is the starting point, not the conclusion. The real work is
verifying whether a pitcher's ERA reflects true talent or borrowed luck.
Pitcher Statcast profiles change throughout the season — a pitcher who
looked legitimate in April may be running on smoke by June, and vice
versa. EVERY starting pitcher on both sides of a matchup must be
verified via Statcast before finalizing any pick, regardless of their
ERA. Never rely on a prior verification from earlier in the season as
current; always pull fresh stats. The three-factor checklist for a
high-confidence pick:

  1. One verified elite starter (clean process stats)
  2. One confirmed regression candidate on the opposing side
  3. Offensive Support: .720+ Season OPS OR Trending Up (Last 10 OPS > .720 or 5+ Runs/Game).

These three factors interact rather than stack. A strong opposing pitcher
or dangerous opposing offense is countervailing pressure that raises the
bar for your pick — it belongs in Stage 1 of the evaluation, not as
additive evidence in your favor. The stronger the resistance on the
opposing side, the more clearly your own edge needs to show through.

Offensive stats are lagging indicators of talent but leading indicators of 
momentum. While season OPS provides the floor, 'Recent Form' (Last 10) 
provides the ceiling. A mismatch in pitching (Elite vs. Regression) is best 
exploited when the offense is in an upswing, regardless of the April 
baseline.


STATCAST VERIFICATION — REQUIRED FOR EVERY STARTING PITCHER
-------------------------------------------------------------
Run this check on BOTH starters before finalizing any pick. Pitcher
stats evolve throughout the season and a prior check — even from earlier
the same week — should not be treated as current. Always pull fresh
numbers. Check the following for each starter:

  • xERA — primary estimator of true run prevention skill
  • FIP — strongest quick indicator of underlying pitching quality;
    isolates strikeouts, walks, and home runs from defense/luck
  • SIERA — advanced contextual ERA estimator; useful confirmation tool
  • xwOBA (expected weighted on-base average)
  • wOBA vs xwOBA gap — key regression signal
  • Barrel % — contact quality confirmation metric
  • Average Exit Velocity (avg EV)
  • Hard Hit % — balls hit 95+ mph

Priority order when time is limited:
  1. xERA vs surface ERA — fastest overall legitimacy check
  2. FIP/SIERA vs surface ERA — confirms whether performance is sustainable
  3. xwOBA gap (wOBA vs xwOBA) — identifies regression direction
  4. Barrel % — supporting confirmation metric, not standalone evidence

xERA and FIP should carry more weight than isolated barrel-rate or
xwOBA readings. A pitcher with strong strikeout/walk fundamentals and
clean ERA estimators can often sustain mildly elevated barrel rates for
extended periods. Barrel rate is most useful as a confirmation signal
rather than the primary driver of a fade.

WHIP remains the fastest surface-level red flag (1.50+ = live regression
trigger) and can be read directly from the CSV without a Statcast search.
But always confirm with xERA/FIP and contact-quality indicators before
acting on WHIP alone.


STRIKEOUT/WALK FOUNDATION
--------------------------
Strikeout and walk ability stabilize faster than many ERA-based metrics
early in the season and should be treated as core indicators of underlying
pitching skill.

Priority metrics:
  • K%
  • BB%
  • K-BB%
  • Swinging Strike Rate (SwStr%)

Guidelines:
  • K-BB% above 18% : Strong underlying skill foundation
  • K-BB% 14% – 18% : Above-average profile
  • K-BB% 10% – 14% : Average; requires supporting contact profile
  • K-BB% below 10% : Elevated risk unless contact suppression is elite
  • BB% above 10%   : Increased collapse risk against patient offenses

Pitchers with elite strikeout ability can sometimes sustain elevated
barrel rates longer than expected. Conversely, low-strikeout pitchers
with weak K-BB profiles have less margin for error when contact quality
deteriorates.



INTERPRETING THE xwOBA GAP
---------------------------
The gap between actual wOBA allowed and xwOBA allowed is the primary
regression predictor.

  POSITIVE gap (wOBA > xwOBA): pitcher has been UNLUCKY — results worse
  than contact quality warrants. Positive regression is coming.
    • ERA is likely overstated; true talent is better than surface ERA shows
    • The pitcher may actually be a pick despite a bad-looking ERA

  NEGATIVE gap (xwOBA > wOBA): pitcher is LUCKY — results better than
  contact quality warrants. Negative regression is coming.
    • Gap -15 to -25: mild luck, ERA will tick upward over the season
    • Gap -25 to -40: meaningful smoke, ERA is somewhat misleading
    • Gap -40 to -60: significant smoke, regression likely within 3-5 starts
    • Gap -60 or more: extreme luck, ERA is essentially fiction

IMPORTANT: Regression predicts seasonal trends, not next-start outcomes.
A pitcher with a +60 gap can still dominate for 2-3 more starts before
correcting. High barrel rate pitchers especially can have isolated
dominant outings. Regression pressure is likely to appear over time, though the exact timing remains uncertain.


BARREL RATE THRESHOLDS
-----------------------
Barrel % is a useful contact-quality indicator, but should be interpreted
alongside xERA, FIP, strikeout ability, and overall run-prevention metrics.
It is most valuable as a supporting confirmation signal rather than a
standalone predictor.

  Below 4%   : Elite contact suppression. Strong supporting indicator.
  4% – 6%    : Very good.
  6% – 9%    : Acceptable range. Evaluate alongside xERA/FIP.
  9% – 12%   : Elevated risk. Requires deeper verification.
  12% – 15%  : Significant danger zone, especially if xERA/FIP also inflated.
  Above 15%  : Extreme contact-risk profile. Usually unsustainable long-term.

IMPORTANT: High barrel rate alone does not automatically make a pitcher
a fade. Pitchers with elite strikeout rates or strong ERA estimators can
sometimes sustain elevated barrel profiles longer than expected.
Conversely, mediocre strikeout pitchers with poor xERA/FIP and high
barrel rates are among the most vulnerable regression candidates.


OFFENSE THRESHOLD FOR REGRESSION FADE PICKS
---------------------------------------------
Picking against a regression candidate (high xERA/FIP indicators,
meaningful xwOBA gap, elevated barrel rate) works most reliably when
the fading team has a strong enough offense to consistently capitalize
on mistakes and sequencing breakdowns.

  Preferred baseline: .720+ team OPS for high-confidence regression fades.

However, the .720 mark should not be treated as an absolute cutoff.
Teams slightly below that threshold can still be viable fade picks when:
  - The opposing starter has severe regression indicators
    (large ERA vs xERA/FIP gap, poor contact profile)
  - The matchup environment is favorable
    (park factors, platoon advantage, weather conditions)
  - The opposing bullpen is weak or overworked
  - The offense has been performing better recently than its season OPS suggests

Below roughly .690 OPS, regression fade picks become substantially less
reliable unless the opposing pitcher profile is genuinely catastrophic.

OPS remains a useful team-level filter, but should be interpreted within
the broader matchup context rather than as a rigid pass/fail rule.


CONVERGENT REGRESSION (COIN FLIP WARNING): Define as a scenario where the perceived pitching edge is shrinking. This occurs when:
Our Pitcher is due for Negative Regression: Surface ERA is lower than xERA/FIP (borrowed luck).
Opposing Pitcher is due for Positive Regression: Surface ERA is higher than xERA/FIP (bad luck).
Rule: If both starters have xwOBA gaps > 15 points moving toward each other (Our Pitcher: Negative gap; Their Pitcher: Positive gap), trigger CONVERGENT status. This is a high-variance fade signal because the talent gap is narrowing, regardless of current ERAs.


WHIP AS A REGRESSION TRIGGER
------------------------------
WHIP above 1.50 is a reliable surface-level flag for a pitcher in trouble.
It reflects high baserunner rates that have not yet fully manifested in
ERA. Against quality offenses (.730+ OPS), high-WHIP pitchers tend to
give up runs when the sequencing stops protecting them.

  1.50 – 1.65 : Elevated risk, particularly against strong offenses
  1.65 – 1.80 : Significant regression trigger
  1.80+        : Extreme — ERA almost certainly understates run risk

Always confirm WHIP regression reads with xwOBA and barrel rate.


RAIN RULES
-----------
Per the pickem rules:
  - Full cancellation/postponement = free win (pick the better team)
  - Delay (not cancellation) = ignore the possibility of rain
  - Confirmed postponement announcement overrides all percentage flags

Roofed stadiums: rain is completely irrelevant. Always note roofed venues
(T-Mobile Park Seattle, Globe Life Field Texas, Tropicana Field Tampa,
Chase Field Arizona, Rogers Centre Toronto, Minute Maid Park Houston,
American Family Field Milwaukee, loanDepot Park Miami) and use them
as tiebreakers when rain is a concern elsewhere.


UNVERIFIED STARTERS — IRON RULE
---------------------------------
NEVER finalize a pick without first running the full Statcast check on
BOTH starting pitchers. This applies to every ERA range, not just
sub-3.00. A 4.50 ERA pitcher with a clean barrel rate and negative
xwOBA gap is a positive regression candidate worth knowing about. A
3.50 ERA pitcher with a +50 xwOBA gap is smoke worth fading.

Sub-3.00 ERA with clean process profile (low barrel rate, low xwOBA
gap) = legitimate ace. The ERA is real. Picking against them requires
overwhelming team quality or regression evidence on the other side.

Sub-3.00 ERA with a high xwOBA gap or elevated barrel rate suggests the
ERA may overstate current performance. Consider fading when matchup
conditions also support it.

High ERA (4.50+) with a negative xwOBA gap and low barrel rate =
unlucky pitcher due for improvement. Don't over-fade them; their
true talent is better than the surface ERA implies.

High ERA (4.50+) with a positive xwOBA gap and high barrel rate usually
indicates a legitimately weak profile. These pitchers are often reasonable
fade candidates.

If a starter's Statcast data is genuinely unavailable (debut, freshly
called up, insufficient MLB sample): treat that matchup as higher-risk.
Consider skipping unless the verified starter on the other side has a
compelling, clear regression or elite profile that stands on its own.
Never assume an unknown arm is either good or bad — treat the unknown
as a variance wildcard that lowers pick confidence by one tier.


PRIOR-SEASON STATCAST PROFILES
--------------------------------
When 2026 Statcast data is unavailable for a starter, the prior season's
contact profile is a legitimate substitute. Contact suppression tendencies
(barrel rate, avg EV, hard-hit rate) are sticky year-over-year. A pitcher
with a 9%+ barrel rate and +30 xwOBA gap in 2025 will very likely carry
similar tendencies into 2026. Use prior-year data with slightly lower
confidence but don't ignore it entirely.


RETURN-FROM-IL AND DEBUT STARTERS
-----------------------------------
Pitchers returning from injury (especially elbow/oblique) or making MLB
debuts are structurally unpredictable. No process stats reliably predict
their performance on a specific game.

Strategy: Pick based on the KNOWN starter on the other side.
  - If the known starter has a verified regression issue → pick that team
    regardless of the unknown arm opposite them
  - If the known starter is verified elite → pick that team even against
    an unknown debut

For return-from-IL starters specifically:
  - Monitor pitch count limits (typically 75-90 pitches early in return)
  - Early exit increases reliance on bullpen → factor bullpen quality
  - Prior-season process stats are the best available proxy


BULLPEN CONSIDERATIONS
-----------------------
Bullpen quality matters most in close games where the starter exits early
or with a narrow lead. Key metrics:

  Average bullpen ERA:
    Under 3.50  : Elite, a genuine closing asset
    3.50 – 4.25 : Solid, reliable in most situations
    4.25 – 5.00 : Below average, notable risk in tight games
    5.00 – 6.00 : Bad, structural liability
    Above 6.00  : Chronic problem, can override pitching advantages
                  in close games and should weigh heavily against picks

Bullpen fatigue: Arms that threw 30+ pitches the previous day are
effectively unavailable. Note how many key relievers pitched yesterday
and at what pitch counts. A bullpen with 4-5 arms used the prior day
is meaningfully weakened regardless of its average ERA.

The Toronto chronic bullpen problem: A team with a 6.00+ ERA bullpen is
structurally difficult to pick even with a great starter. The drag is
meaningful but not an automatic disqualifier — weigh it against starter
projected innings, opposing offense level, and whether the opposing
bullpen is equally compromised.


TEAM QUALITY FLOOR
-------------------
Elite teams (top 5 in record, .780+ OPS, sub-3.50 bullpen ERA) have a
floor that absorbs bad pitching performances. They find ways to win
even when their starter struggles.

Indicators of a reliable team quality floor:
  • Season win percentage above .620
  • Team OPS above .780
  • Bullpen average ERA below 3.50
  • 7-3 or better in their last 10

Teams meeting all four criteria should receive a substantial confidence
boost when facing confirmed regression candidates, even if their own
starter is mediocre. The offense and bullpen will compensate.

Teams with elite records but weak offenses (.680 OPS or lower despite
good records) do NOT have the same floor. Their wins are likely
pitching-driven, making them more vulnerable to variance.


GAME-SELECTION HIERARCHY
--------------------------
In order of confidence:

TIER 1 — HIGH CONFIDENCE (pick these)
  • Verified elite starter (low barrel rate, clean xwOBA, low FIP/xERA)
    vs confirmed regression candidate (high xwOBA gap or high barrel rate)
  • Opposing offense has .720+ OPS to cash the edge

TIER 2 — MODERATE CONFIDENCE (consider with caveats)
  • Strong team quality floor facing unverified but plausible bad pitcher
  • Win streak team with a WHIP trigger on opposing starter
  • Both starters verified decent but one team has meaningfully better
    bullpen and offense

TIER 3 — SPECULATIVE (approach with caution)
  • Picking against high barrel rate pitcher with team below .720 OPS
  • Unverified starters on either side
  • Both starters have active opposing xwOBA gaps (bidirectional coin flip)

SKIP THESE:
  • Both starters have ERA above 5.50 (genuine mess, unpredictable)
  • Both starters have WHIP above 1.60 (bidirectional regression bomb)
  • Two bad teams (both below .450 win pct) with no process edge

CONFIDENCE EVALUATION — OPPOSING SIDE PRESSURE THEN EDGE SCORING
------------------------------------------------------------------
Evaluation runs in two stages. Stage 1 assesses how much resistance the
opposing side presents. Stage 2 scores the strength of the edge on the
side being picked. A strong team or strong pitcher on the opposing side
is NOT additive evidence for your pick — it is countervailing pressure
that belongs in Stage 1, not Stage 2. Nothing in Stage 1 automatically
kills a pick; everything is a signal to weigh against your edge.


STAGE 1 — OPPOSING SIDE PRESSURE (check what you're picking against)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Each condition below raises the bar for the edge you need in Stage 2.
No single condition here is a hard stop. Instead, count how many apply
and consider how severely — more pressure requires a stronger edge to
justify the pick.

PITCHER RESISTANCE
  • Opposing starter is verified elite (xERA sub-3.00, barrel below 6%,
    neutral or negative xwOBA gap). A legitimate ace significantly raises
    the bar. A regression-fade pick into a verified ace requires either
    an overwhelming team quality edge or clear smoke on your own starter's
    side. The cleaner the opposing profile, the less justified the fade.
  • Opposing starter is moderate-quality but clean (xERA 3.00–3.75,
    barrel below 9%, neutral gap). Real resistance — your edge needs to
    be clear and multi-layered, not marginal.

TEAM RESISTANCE
  • Opposing team OPS above .780. Elite offenses can overcome bad pitching
    performances and neutralize regression advantages. The higher above
    .780, the more this matters.
  • Opposing team is 7-3 or better in their last 10 AND has a .750+ OPS.
    Hot, dangerous teams are running well across all phases and absorb
    unfavorable game scripts.
  • Opposing bullpen ERA below 3.00. An elite pen preserves narrow leads
    even when the starter struggles, compressing the ceiling on a
    regression outcome.

STRUCTURAL UNCERTAINTY
  • Bidirectional regression: both starters have active xwOBA gaps above
    15 points in opposing directions. Whichever pitcher corrects first
    determines the game — team quality is a weaker signal here.
  • Your side's starter is unverified (NaN, debut, or insufficient sample).
    Picking based on the opposing regression candidate alone is viable but
    confidence should be lower.
  • Both starters unverified. No process edge can be established on either
    side. Requires an unusually strong team quality argument to proceed.

After inventorying Stage 1 pressure, proceed to Stage 2. The more Stage 1
resistance present, the stronger your Stage 2 edge needs to be to reach
High or Moderate confidence.


STAGE 2 — EDGE SCORING (evaluate the side being picked)
--------------------------------------------------------
Score only the strength of the edge on your side. This is not cumulative
quality — it measures how clearly the conditions favor a win for your pick.

PITCHING EDGE (opposing starter's vulnerability)
  Strong  : Opposing starter has xwOBA gap above +40, barrel above 12%,
            or xERA more than 1.5 runs above surface ERA. Clear fade target.
  Moderate: Opposing starter has xwOBA gap +15 to +40, barrel 9–12%,
            or xERA 0.75–1.50 above surface ERA. Real but not extreme edge.
  Weak    : Opposing starter has mild indicators only. Edge exists but
            is not the primary reason for the pick.

OFFENSE THRESHOLD (can your side cash the edge?)
  Strong  : Team OPS above .740. Reliably punishes regression and
            sequencing breakdowns.
  Adequate: Team OPS .700–.740. Can cash the edge but less consistently.
  Marginal: Team OPS .690–.700. Below the reliable threshold; regression
            fade picks become substantially less dependable.
  Insufficient: Team OPS below .690. Do not use as the primary basis
            for a regression fade unless opposing pitcher profile is
            genuinely catastrophic.

SUPPORTING CONTEXT
  Boost : Team is 7-3 or better last 10, or on a W3+ streak. Recent
          form adds floor when pitching edge is moderate.
  Boost : Your bullpen ERA below 3.50. Holds narrow leads; reduces
          variance in close games.
  Drain : Your bullpen ERA above 5.50. Structural liability that can
          erase a starter advantage.
  Drain : 4+ bullpen arms used yesterday. Meaningful fatigue regardless
          of season ERA.
  Drain : Your team on an L4+ streak. Negative momentum is real.

CONFIDENCE TIERS (based on Stage 2 edge vs Stage 1 pressure)
  High      : Strong pitching edge AND strong offense threshold, with
              at least one supporting boost, no drains, and light
              Stage 1 pressure (no verified elite arm, no elite offense).
  Moderate  : Strong pitching edge with marginal offense, OR moderate
              pitching edge with strong offense. Some Stage 1 pressure
              present but clearly outweighed by the edge.
  Low       : Weak pitching edge, one or more active drains, or
              meaningful Stage 1 pressure that is only partially offset.
              Proceed cautiously or look for a better game.
  Skip      : No clear edge can be established, or Stage 1 pressure is
              heavy on multiple dimensions with only a marginal Stage 2
              edge to show against it. Includes both starters unverified
              with no strong team quality argument.

The tier reflects the net balance of edge versus resistance, not the
absolute quality of either team. A moderate edge into light resistance
can be High confidence. A strong edge into heavy resistance may only
reach Moderate. When Stage 1 pressure is severe, ask honestly whether
the edge is real enough to overcome it — if the answer is uncertain,
treat it as Low or Skip.


SUMMARY CHECKLIST — PRE-GAME VERIFICATION
-------------------------------------------
Before finalizing any pick, work through Stage 1 then Stage 2.

STAGE 1 — OPPOSING SIDE PRESSURE (inventory what you're picking against)
  □ Is the opposing starter verified elite? (xERA sub-3.00, barrel below
    6%, neutral or negative xwOBA gap) → Significant resistance. Note it
    and assess whether your Stage 2 edge is strong enough to justify.
  □ Is the opposing starter moderate-quality verified? (xERA 3.00–3.75,
    barrel below 9%) → Real but manageable resistance. Note it.
  □ Is the opposing team OPS above .780? → Raises the bar on offense
    threshold needed in Stage 2.
  □ Is the opposing team 7-3 or better last 10 AND above .750 OPS?
    → Hot team running on all phases. Note it.
  □ Is the opposing bullpen ERA below 3.00? → Compresses regression
    ceiling. Note it.
  □ Is this a convergent regression game? (both starters' xwOBA gaps
    above 15 pts in opposing directions) → Elevated variance. Coin-flip
    risk — requires unusually strong team quality argument to proceed.
  □ The "Juggernaut" Safety Valve (Avoid the Trap). Teams like the Dodgers 
    often have "limitless" offensive ceilings.
  □ Is your starter unverified? → Lowers confidence regardless of
    opposing regression evidence. Note it.
  □ Are both starters unverified? → No process edge. Requires strong
    team quality argument. Lean toward skipping.

STAGE 2 — EDGE SCORING (your side)
  □ Both starters fully verified via fresh Statcast search?
    (required for ALL starters — stats change throughout the season;
    prior checks are not current)
  □ Opposing starter's xwOBA gap assessed (strong/moderate/weak edge)
  □ Opposing starter's barrel rate and xERA gap assessed
  □ WHIP on opposing starter noted (1.50+ = additional fade flag)
  □ Your team OPS assessed (strong/adequate/marginal/insufficient)
  □ Your team's recent form noted (7-3 or better = boost)
  □ Your bullpen ERA and yesterday's usage checked (drain if 3+ 
    high-leverage arms pitched yesterday (closer, primary setup, 
    key bridge relievers))
  □ Your team's streak noted (L4+ = drain)
  □ Rain flag checked — above 40%? If so, check NWS game-window
  □ Is this a roofed venue? (weather irrelevant if yes)
  □ Are either starters returning from IL or making MLB debuts?
    (treat as higher variance — pick based on the known arm)
