MLB DAILY PICKEM FRAMEWORK
================================================================

CORE PHILOSOPHY
---------------
Surface ERA is the starting point, not the conclusion. The real work is
verifying whether a pitcher's ERA reflects true talent or borrowed luck.
EVERY starting pitcher on both sides must be verified via Statcast before
finalizing any pick — stats evolve throughout the season and prior checks
are never current. Always pull fresh numbers.

Evaluation runs in two stages. Stage 1 inventories how much resistance
the opposing side presents. Stage 2 scores the strength of the edge on
the side being picked. A strong opposing pitcher or offense is
countervailing pressure that belongs in Stage 1 — it is NOT additive
evidence for your pick. The stronger the resistance, the more clearly
your edge needs to show through.


STATCAST VERIFICATION — REQUIRED FOR EVERY STARTING PITCHER
-------------------------------------------------------------
Check the following for each starter (both sides, every game):

  • xERA — primary estimator of true run prevention skill
  • FIP — isolates K, BB, HR from defense/luck; strongest quick check
  • xwOBA and wOBA vs xwOBA gap — primary regression signal
  • Barrel % — contact quality confirmation
  • WHIP — fastest surface red flag (1.50+ = live regression trigger)

Priority when time is limited: xERA vs ERA → FIP/SIERA vs ERA →
xwOBA gap → Barrel %. xERA and FIP carry more weight than barrel rate
alone. Barrel rate is a confirmation signal, not a standalone fade driver.


STRIKEOUT/WALK FOUNDATION
--------------------------
K-BB% stabilizes faster than ERA-based metrics and is a core skill signal.

  • K-BB% above 18% : Strong foundation
  • K-BB% 14–18%    : Above average
  • K-BB% 10–14%    : Average; requires clean contact profile
  • K-BB% below 10% : Elevated risk
  • BB% above 10%   : Collapse risk against patient offenses

Elite strikeout ability can sustain elevated barrel rates longer than
expected. Low-K pitchers with poor K-BB% have no margin for error.


INTERPRETING THE xwOBA GAP
---------------------------
Gap = wOBA allowed minus xwOBA allowed.

  POSITIVE gap (wOBA > xwOBA): pitcher UNLUCKY — ERA overstates true risk.
  May be a pick despite a bad-looking surface ERA.

  NEGATIVE gap (xwOBA > wOBA): pitcher LUCKY — ERA understates true risk.
    -15 to -25 : Mild luck
    -25 to -40 : Meaningful smoke
    -40 to -60 : Significant smoke, regression likely within 3–5 starts
    -60+       : ERA is essentially fiction

Regression predicts seasonal trends, not next-start outcomes. A pitcher
with a large gap can still dominate for 2–3 more starts before correcting.

CONVERGENT REGRESSION (COIN FLIP): If both starters have xwOBA gaps
above 15 points moving toward each other (your pitcher: negative gap;
their pitcher: positive gap), the talent difference is narrowing. This
is a high-variance situation regardless of current ERAs — treat as a
coin flip and require a strong team quality argument to proceed.


BARREL RATE THRESHOLDS
-----------------------
  Below 4%  : Elite contact suppression
  4–6%      : Very good
  6–9%      : Acceptable; evaluate alongside xERA/FIP
  9–12%     : Elevated risk
  12–15%    : Significant danger zone
  Above 15% : Extreme; usually unsustainable

High barrel rate alone is not a fade trigger. Pitchers with elite K rates
can sustain elevated barrel profiles. Mediocre K pitchers with poor
xERA/FIP and high barrel rates are the most vulnerable regression targets.


WHIP AS A REGRESSION TRIGGER
------------------------------
  1.50–1.65 : Elevated risk against strong offenses
  1.65–1.80 : Significant regression trigger
  1.80+     : Extreme — ERA almost certainly understates run risk

Always confirm WHIP reads with xwOBA gap and barrel rate before acting.


OFFENSE EVALUATION (PLATOON-ADJUSTED)
--------------------------------------
The primary offense question is how well this lineup hits against the
opposing starter's handedness. You will be provided the top 6 batters
per team by OPS, along with their OPS vs. RHP and vs. LHP.

STEP 1 — SEASON OPS (CONTEXT ANCHOR)
Note the team's overall season OPS as a baseline. This is not the
primary driver — a team with a .700 overall OPS may be a .760 lineup
vs. RHP and a .640 lineup vs. LHP, making overall OPS largely
irrelevant to the actual matchup. Use it only to flag extreme cases
(.650 or below = structurally weak offense regardless of splits;
.800+ = elite floor that holds even in unfavorable platoon situations)
and as a sanity check against noisy split samples early in the season.

STEP 2 — PLATOON OPS ASSESSMENT
Identify the opposing starter's handedness (RHP or LHP), then read
each of the top 6 batters' OPS against that hand. Compute or estimate
the weighted average, weighting toward the top of the lineup. This
number is the primary offense tier assignment.

  Strong      : Avg OPS vs. that hand .740+. Reliably punishes
                regression and sequencing breakdowns.
  Adequate    : Avg OPS vs. that hand .700–.740. Can cash the edge
                but less consistently.
  Marginal    : Avg OPS vs. that hand .690–.700. Regression fades
                become substantially less dependable.
  Insufficient: Avg OPS vs. that hand below .690. Do not use as the
                primary basis for a regression fade unless the opposing
                pitcher profile is genuinely catastrophic.

STEP 3 — INDIVIDUAL SPLIT ALIGNMENT
After assessing the group average, flag notable individual mismatches:

  Favorable   : 3+ of the top 6 batters have .800+ OPS vs. that hand.
                Strong platoon alignment; boosts confidence in cashing
                the pitching edge.
  Unfavorable : 3+ of the top 6 batters have .650 OPS or below vs. that
                hand. Meaningful platoon liability; downgrade offense one
                tier, especially if the pitcher has strong platoon splits.
  Neutral     : Mixed results. No material adjustment.

Individual batter splits can be noisy in small samples. Weight the
group average more heavily than any single outlier batter. When split
samples are thin (early season), lean on overall season OPS as a
stabilizing reference.

STEP 4 — RECENT FORM ADJUSTMENT
Season splits are lagging indicators. If a team's last-10 OPS or
recent run scoring is meaningfully above their seasonal platoon
average, that trajectory is worth noting as a ceiling signal —
particularly when the seasonal average is borderline adequate.


UNVERIFIED AND SPECIAL-CASE STARTERS
--------------------------------------
  • Debut or insufficient MLB sample: Treat as a variance wildcard.
    Lower confidence by one tier. Pick based on the known arm.
  • Return from IL: No process stats reliably predict performance.
    Monitor pitch count limits (75–90 pitches early in return). Use
    prior-season contact profile as the best available proxy.
  • Prior-season Statcast: When 2026 data is unavailable, prior-year
    contact tendencies (barrel rate, avg EV, hard-hit %) are sticky
    year-over-year and are a legitimate lower-confidence substitute.

ERA interpretation shortcuts:
  • Sub-3.00 ERA + clean profile (low barrel, neutral/negative gap) =
    legitimate ace. Picking against requires overwhelming evidence.
  • Sub-3.00 ERA + high xwOBA gap or elevated barrel = smoke. Fadeable
    when matchup conditions support it.
  • High ERA (4.50+) + negative gap + low barrel = unlucky, due for
    improvement. Don't over-fade.
  • High ERA (4.50+) + positive gap + high barrel = legitimately weak.
    Reasonable fade candidate.


BULLPEN CONSIDERATIONS
-----------------------
  Under 3.50  : Elite closing asset
  3.50–4.25   : Solid
  4.25–5.00   : Below average; notable risk in tight games
  5.00–6.00   : Structural liability
  Above 6.00  : Chronic problem; can override starter advantages

Fatigue: Note how many key relievers (closer, primary setup, key bridge)
pitched yesterday. 3+ high-leverage arms used = meaningful drain
regardless of season ERA.


RAIN RULES
-----------
  • Full postponement = free win (pick the better team)
  • Delay only = ignore rain as a factor
  • Confirmed postponement overrides all probability flags

Roofed venues (rain irrelevant): T-Mobile Park, Globe Life Field,
Tropicana Field, Chase Field, Rogers Centre, Minute Maid Park,
American Family Field, loanDepot Park.

Use roofed venues as tiebreakers when rain is a concern elsewhere.

STAGE 1 — OPPOSING SIDE PRESSURE
================================================================
Inventory what you're picking against. Nothing here is an automatic
stop — count how many apply and how severely. More pressure requires
a stronger Stage 2 edge to justify the pick.

PITCHER RESISTANCE
  • Verified elite (xERA sub-3.00, barrel below 6%, neutral/negative gap):
    Significant bar-raiser. Requires overwhelming team edge or clear smoke
    on your starter to proceed.
  • Moderate-quality verified (xERA 3.00–3.75, barrel below 9%):
    Real resistance. Your edge needs to be clear, not marginal.

TEAM RESISTANCE
  • Opposing team platoon OPS above .780 vs. your starter's hand:
    Elite offenses neutralize regression advantages.
  • Opposing team 7-3 or better last 10 AND .750+ platoon OPS:
    Hot team running on all phases.
  • Opposing bullpen ERA below 3.00: Compresses regression ceiling even
    when the starter struggles.
  • "Juggernaut" teams (Dodgers etc.): Offensive ceiling is effectively
    limitless. Treat with extra caution.

STRUCTURAL UNCERTAINTY
  • Convergent regression (both xwOBA gaps above 15 pts in opposing
    directions): Coin-flip variance. Strong team quality argument required.
  • Your starter unverified: Lower confidence by one tier regardless of
    opposing regression evidence.
  • Both starters unverified: No process edge. Lean toward skipping.

STAGE 2 — EDGE SCORING (your side)
================================================================
Score only the strength of the edge on the side you're picking.

PITCHING EDGE
  Strong  : Opposing starter xwOBA gap above +40, barrel above 12%,
            or xERA more than 1.5 runs above surface ERA.
  Moderate: xwOBA gap +15 to +40, barrel 9–12%, or xERA 0.75–1.50
            above surface ERA.
  Weak    : Mild indicators only. Edge exists but is not the primary
            reason for the pick.

OFFENSE EDGE (platoon-adjusted — see OFFENSE EVALUATION section)
  Strong      : Avg OPS vs. pitcher hand .740+
  Adequate    : Avg OPS vs. pitcher hand .700–.740
  Marginal    : Avg OPS vs. pitcher hand .690–.700
  Insufficient: Avg OPS vs. pitcher hand below .690

  Apply individual alignment modifier (Step 2) to upgrade or downgrade
  one tier based on how many of the top 6 batters are favorable or
  unfavorable vs. that hand.

SUPPORTING CONTEXT
  Boost : Team 7-3 or better last 10, or W3+ streak
  Boost : Your bullpen ERA below 3.50
  Drain : Your bullpen ERA above 5.50
  Drain : 3+ high-leverage arms used yesterday
  Drain : Your team on L4+ streak

CONFIDENCE TIERS
  High     : Strong pitching edge + strong offense, at least one boost,
             no drains, light Stage 1 pressure.
  Moderate : Strong pitching edge with marginal offense, OR moderate
             edge with strong offense. Some Stage 1 pressure but clearly
             outweighed.
  Low      : Weak pitching edge, active drains, or Stage 1 pressure only
             partially offset. Proceed cautiously.
  Skip     : No clear edge, or heavy Stage 1 pressure with only a marginal
             Stage 2 edge. Both starters unverified with no team quality
             argument.
